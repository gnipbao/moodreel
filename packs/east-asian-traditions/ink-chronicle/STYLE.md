# Ink Chronicle — Style Prompt

Ink Chronicle · 墨字列传

> Type as the world: characters set on paper and on night become a film's ground, objects and weather. They land like
> blows, gather out of grit and break back into it, while a camera travels among them; one warm accent marks what
> matters. Everything else is the film's to invent.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

The signature, in five traits. A frame that keeps them reads as this style, whatever its subject:

- **Words are the world.** Type is the picture's matter, not a caption over it: a field of characters, a floor of
  them, a character standing as an object, a single word filling the frame.
- **Paper, night and one accent.** Achromatic grounds carry the film; one warm colour marks one thing at a time.
- **Words land like blows.** What matters arrives already full and too big, and settles in an instant.
- **Matter becomes words, words become matter.** Grit, ink, sand, shards, creatures: the text turns into things and
  back.
- **A camera that travels in the world of type**, and transitions that are the next shot seen from elsewhere.

Not Chinese Ink Wash (no loaded brushstrokes on empty paper; here the type is set and is the subject). Not Swiss Motion
(no grid; things land, shatter and fly). Not a lyric video (the text's sense leads, not a beat).

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Two grounds**: paper (warm, clean, quiet: the calm voice) and night (near-black or charcoal, lit by one source:
  where fear, judgement and solitude live). A film may add its own: mist, a coloured field for one beat, a material
  of its subject.
- **Type is matter** and can take any state made of its own marks: solid, grit gathered from its strokes, speckled
  like chalk or snow, cracked into shards, carved on blocks, printed across a surface, piled up, poured, woven. Invent
  others (wet ink, embers, smoke, stitches) as long as they come from the letterforms.
- **Pictures are sparse** and made of the same marks as the type: stipple, grit, a flat wash, streaks, rows of small
  waves, a few planes. What they show is the film's: there is no stock imagery in this style.
- **Space is welcome**: text on any surface in 3D, characters standing as objects, a page in the air; or none, if the
  film lives flat.
- **Light blooms**: white type on night, a lamp or a moon, a lit word carry a soft glow, and the frame's edges fall
  away into shadow, strongly by night. The paper stays smooth and the grain holds still: only what moves, moves.

## 3. Colour logic

- **Grounds are achromatic** and change with the scene; most of the film has no colour.
- **One accent** (an orange-red in the reference, e.g. #fd5e23) is spent on one meaning at a time: fame, defiance, a
  charge, a binding, a judgement, a route. Its hue is the film's choice; its scarcity is the style's.
- **Colour leaving is an event**: a mark draining to grey as its meaning is lost. The accent may fill the frame for a
  beat or two.
- Ink black on paper (e.g. #16130f), near-white on night (e.g. #f4f2ee).

## 4. Type & subtitles

- **Four voices in three classes**: a heavy Song for blows, titles and lines that address the viewer; the same Song,
  light, for the quiet modern voice; an upright Kai (or Fangsong) for quotations and reflection; a mono for numbers,
  dates and distances. Defaults: Noto Serif SC, LXGW WenKai, IBM Plex Mono; any open faces of these classes will do.
- **Scale contrast is the instrument**: a blow can fill a third of the frame or more beside labels a twentieth of
  its size; quotations sit near a tenth.
- Classical lines tend to stand in vertical columns, the modern voice to run horizontally; a small running label
  (place, year, distance) can keep the film's index in a corner.
- The text is its own subtitle. Lines meant to be read hold at least CJK characters / 4.5 + 1.5 s; a blow can be
  brief.

## 5. Motion quality

- **The slam** is the signature: a word arrives at about twice its size, full on its first frame, and settles in a
  few frames (measured 0.08–0.1 s), easing out. On a dark ground its colours can split; the hardest blow can flash its
  negative. Spend it on what matters.
- **The voice types**, a character at a time: quick for the modern voice, slower for a quotation.
- **Matter has physics**: grit gathers and falls, shards drop and turn, a line loses its hold and jumbles in place,
  repeated rows tremble. Each particle comes from something and becomes something.
- Movement is brisk: entrances around a third of a second with a slight overshoot, exits a little shorter and often
  forceful (flown out, dropped, shattered, or gone with the light).
- Ambient life (rain, drifting specks, glints, wings, a walker's step) is welcome. Frame rate and stepping are the
  film's (the reference runs 30 fps on ones).

## 6. Camera grammar

The camera behaves like the type: it waits, then moves with intent, and it travels **through** the world of the text
rather than past it. Its kinds of move, each with what it tends to express (the reference's uses are only examples):

| Kind of move | What it tends to express | As the reference used it (an example, not a shot to repeat) |
|---|---|---|
| Pulling away from one word into many | one among many | off a name into a field of type |
| Moving through the medium | travelling through the text, entering it | low over a floor of words; through standing characters |
| A push that lands like a blow | the one thing that matters | into a peak until it whites out |
| Holding, then leaving with gathering pace | distance, reflection | back off a moon |
| Closing or opening on a detail | confinement, a turn | an iris on a pair of cords |
| Letting the words drop out of frame | a moment passing | a year sliding away as the camera rises |

Compositions tend to be centred and full-bleed. Transitions: a cut on the text's sense, a dissolve, the next shot seen
from elsewhere, a shape opening onto the next picture, one white frame on an impact, or the film's own. Shot lengths
and cut rhythm follow the film's text and music.

## 7. Sound palette

Open. The reference's spare acoustic texture suits the style (a few plucked or struck notes, low strings, a low hit
under each blow, the matter's foley: paper, an ink drop, stone, rain, wind, sand), and a film may take a pulse, a
choir or electronic textures instead. Narration, if any, stays close and lets the type lead. Mix at −14 LUFS.

## 8. Native moves

The style's own moments come from a few principles. Each has one example from the reference; invent the film's own.

- **The text becomes what it names.** A river's words flow, a mountain's are stippled into its slope, a crowd is a
  field of names. *The reference:* a character's grit gathered into a goose that flew. *Fits:* a metaphor; a place;
  a thing that is its name.
- **An arrival is a blow.** The word that matters is already there, too big, and settles at once; the hardest one may
  flash its negative. *The reference:* a year landing with its colours split. *Fits:* a date; a verdict; a price.
- **Colour is a meaning, and losing it an event.** The accent marks one thing; it drains when the meaning goes. *The
  reference:* a tile of fame going grey. *Fits:* a fall; a broken promise; a lost title.
- **Matter and words turn into each other.** Words gather out of grit, sand, smoke or ink and go back to it; they
  crack, fall, pile up. *The reference:* a word shattering and falling out of the frame. *Fits:* a memory; a claim
  dissolving; a record broken.
- **A count or a route as type.** Numbers count in the mono; places are marks on a line. *The reference:* distances
  counting up between places. *Fits:* a career; a migration; a supply chain.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- A slam that fades in loses its blow; a fade where the style would slam reads as hesitation.
- Colour everywhere spends the accent: it only means something when it is rare.
- Particles from nowhere read as an effect; give them a source and a destination.
- 3D characters with light sides go grey when the camera flies through them: give them dark sides.
- Grey type at low contrast (under about 15 %) vanishes in H.264 at caption sizes.
- A reference's text is never a film's corpus, and its set pieces (ANALYSIS.md) are never re-staged: each film brings
  its own words and invents its own scenes.

## 10. Engine

The kit gives the signature's primitives, none of them a scene: `kit/tokens.js` (grounds, ink, accent, voices, slam and
typing values), `kit/motion.js` (measured timings), `kit/faces.js` (the three faces and `loadFaces()`), `kit/paper.js`
(paper, night, label, flash), `kit/type.js` (`typed()` with `slam` and `split`, `slam()`, `textPoints()`, `dots()`,
`speckle()`, `shatter()`, `jumble()`, `rows()`, `typeField()` over any region or polygon), `kit/marks.js` (`stipple()`,
`wash()`, `waves()`, `streaks()`, `glow()`, each over any shape or band), `kit/space.js` (`typeSpace()`: text on any plane,
characters as blocks, the style's light and haze; `fitCamera()`).

A minimal example of a scene the reference doesn't have, a mountain made of its own name:

```js
paper(g);
typeField(g, t, { corpus, shape: [[0.2 * W, 0.85 * H], [0.5 * W, 0.25 * H], [0.8 * W, 0.85 * H]], dens: [[0, 0], [1.2, 1]], cell: 28 });
typed(g, '山', 0.5 * W, 0.2 * H, t, 0, { at: [1.4], size: 0.3 * H, weight: 900, align: 'center', slam: 2 });
```

## 11. Variation space

Everything outside §1 is the film's: subject and corpus, length and aspect, the order of grounds, the accent's hue,
structure, music, narration, how much imagery and whether it is 2D or 3D. Chinese is the native script; another script
can carry the style if its type can bear the same weight. Unseen in the reference and open: a vertical frame, a map,
photographs turned to grit, two voices in dialogue, a score with a pulse, colour grounds for a chapter.

- Structures, for instance: a life in dates and places; a journey from source to end; an argument in quotations
  answered by the modern voice; a single word's history.
- Openings, for instance: one word slammed on black, becoming what it names; a date typed into the dark before the
  first blow; one line of the text crossing an empty frame like a road.
- Endings, for instance: a route complete, its places lit; the last word turning into its matter; a field of type
  thinning until one character is left.
